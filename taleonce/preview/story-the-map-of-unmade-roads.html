A Road for One Day

Anya could draw a road that existed for one day. At dawn the ink left the page and the path appeared on earth. At the next dawn it vanished, carrying away whatever had not reached the far side. Her master had taught her to use the gift only for emergencies: a bridge around a landslide, a trail to a missing shepherd, a way home for someone caught by floodwater. Every road had a cost. The ink came from stone ground with a memory of motion, and when one path was made, another nearby grew difficult to find.

Anya thought that warning meant a traveler might take a wrong turn. She had never seen a path disappear completely until the king summoned her.

The summons arrived while she was repairing a county map. King Aric wanted a road across the Blackglass Valley, where the mountain fell in sharp shelves and no ordinary track survived the winter. His army needed to cross before a rebel force reached the northern forts. He offered her a title, a house, and enough money to restore the mapmakers' hall. He did not ask what the road would take.

Anya traveled to the capital with her master, Sol, and a pack mule carrying paper, stone ink, and the survey instruments. At the palace she was shown a campaign map. The requested road was a straight line from the south gate to the valley's far ridge. It would save the army nine days. Sol placed a finger on the small villages along the old mountain route. 'Where do those people go if this new line takes theirs?'

The minister said the villages were almost empty. Anya asked to see the population ledger. The minister looked annoyed, then promised it tomorrow. That night a stable boy slipped a note under her door. His family lived in Sere, the first village marked almost empty. Seventy-six people lived there. They had stored enough food to last the winter and expected the old pass to remain open. If Anya's road displaced it, they would be trapped behind the ridge with no safe descent.
